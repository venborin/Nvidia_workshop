The N-body problem in electronic structure

The time-independent Schrödinger equation for N electrons in the field of clamped nuclei:

Ĥ |Ψ⟩ = E |Ψ⟩

with the Hamiltonian in second quantization:

Ĥ = ∑pq hpq a†p aq + ¼ ∑pqrs ⟨pq||rs⟩ a†p a†q as ar

The Hilbert space dimension grows factorially with system size. Coupled cluster tames this by writing the exact wavefunction as an exponential of excitation operators acting on a single Slater determinant |Φ₀⟩ (usually Hartree–Fock):

|ΨCC⟩ = eT̂ |Φ₀⟩,   T̂ = T̂₁ + T̂₂ + T̂₃ + …

where T̂₁ = ∑ia tia a†a ai (singles), T̂₂ = ¼ ∑ijab tijab a†a a†b aj ai (doubles), etc.

Why does the exponential ansatz eT̂ guarantee size extensivity, whereas a linear CI expansion (1 + T̂₁ + T̂₂ + …) does not?

The linked-cluster theorem

The energy is obtained by projecting the Schrödinger equation onto the reference:

E = ⟨Φ₀| e−T̂ Ĥ eT̂ |Φ₀⟩ = ⟨Φ₀| Ĥ̄ |Φ₀⟩

The similarity-transformed Hamiltonian Ĥ̄ = e−T̂ Ĥ eT̂ expands via the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff (BCH) formula. Because Ĥ contains at most two-body operators and T̂ is a sum of excitation operators, the BCH series terminates exactly at four nested commutators:

Ĥ̄ = Ĥ + [Ĥ, T̂] + ½ [[Ĥ, T̂], T̂] + ⅙ [[[Ĥ, T̂], T̂], T̂] + ¹/₂⁴ [[[[Ĥ, T̂], T̂], T̂], T̂]

Only linked (connected) diagrams survive in the expectation value ⟨Φ₀|Ĥ̄|Φ₀⟩. Unlinked diagrams cancel diagram-by-diagram—this is the linked-cluster theorem. It is the algebraic reason CC is size-extensive.

The BCH expansion of e−T̂ Ĥ eT̂ terminates at four commutators. What property of Ĥ and T̂ makes this happen, and why would it fail if Ĥ contained three-body operators?
